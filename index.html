What the frequency data shows

  • Retargeting reaches the same people 15.03 times in 41 days, about 11 times a month, across 715,716 people.
  • Past customers are seeing ads 26.13 times in 41 days, about 19 times a month, from a pool of 286,312 people; this campaign alone produced 5,576 purchases, so it is converting existing demand rather than finding new buyers.
  • Non-retargeting frequency is also high at 9.17, because the largest "prospecting" campaigns (Adv+ _Past at 6.83, Featured Favorites at 6.85, DPA at 5.35) are re-serving a limited audience instead of reaching new people.
  • The two groups produce almost the same number of purchases (6,670 retargeting against 6,764 non-retargeting), even though retargeting reaches about half as many people, which confirms how much of Meta volume comes from people already in the funnel.
  • Recommended cap: past-customer retargeting at 8 a month and prospecting below 3 a month, with reach growth tracked as a KPI.

What the add-to-cart data shows

  • Adds to cart exceeded landing page views in 19 of 22 months; the rate peaked at 235% in February 2026 (136,217 adds to cart from 58,039 landing page views).
  • Full-year 2025 ran at 135%, so the issue predates 2026 and is structural rather than a one-off.
  • Only about 4% of reported adds to cart become purchases in every month, far below what an event firing once per real add would produce.
  • The rate dipped to 81% to 95% in June, August and September 2026 as landing page views rose after the tracking change, and returned to 105% in early October, so the add-to-cart event has not been fixed.

Where it is worst

  • Existing customers show the highest over-reporting: 182% in 2025 and 159% in 2026, which suggests the event fires repeatedly for returning visitors (for example on every size or quantity change, or from both the pixel and the Conversions API without de-duplication).
  • Prospecting is lower but still implausible at 90% in 2025 and 64% in 2026.

Key findings

  • New-customer acquisition is the core problem. First-time orders fell 21% while returning orders fell 15%, and paid spend per new customer rose from $37 to $46.
  • 60% of Meta spend reaches people who have already engaged or purchased. Past-customer retargeting alone reached 37% of Meta spend in September.
  • March and April 2026 were the largest single leak. Up to 87% of Meta budget ran in one Advantage+ campaign at 0.8x to 2.0x, with no prospecting campaigns live.
  • Google wastes budget on non-converting search terms. 58% of visible search-term spend since June produced no conversions, and Standard Shopping has returned 1.63x on $581K since late 2024.
  • The strongest assets are under-funded. GIF creative (4.8x), Advantage+ (5.9x to 7.3x since July), top-100 best sellers on PMax (5.7x) and the TikTok UGC test (5.9x on clicks).

Why returning customers now carry a larger share

Paid media mix

  • Only 40% of Meta spend from January to September reached people new to the brand; 60% reached people who had already engaged with or purchased from Marie Nicole (21% engaged, 39% existing customers).
  • With Meta at 85% of paid spend, this allocation is a significant reason the order mix tilted towards returning customers, and a significant reason total revenue fell: re-reaching existing buyers recycles demand the store already had instead of adding new customers.

Meta overview

Excluding brand-awareness ads, Meta spent 5% more in January to September 2026 than a year earlier and reported 28% less purchase value, so ROAS fell from 4.9x to 3.4x. Meta is 85% of paid spend, which makes it the main paid-media driver of the 2026 store decline.
